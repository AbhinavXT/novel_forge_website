The premise

Built for people who actually read.

Most reading apps assume you read in 10-minute commutes. This one doesn't. It's built for the person who finishes a series in a weekend and starts another the same night. The kind of reader who keeps half a dozen books in active rotation and remembers which paragraph they stopped at three days later.

That means offline by default, because you read in the subway and on flights. Eleven themes, because your eyes change as the day goes on. Eight fonts, because some days a serif feels right and some days it doesn't. Stats you can look at and think "huh, I read more than I thought," instead of stats that get sent to a dashboard somewhere.

The whole thing is free. The source is open. Whatever's on your phone stays there.

The product

Designed like the books it carries.

The app's been built and rebuilt over the past year, mostly on weekends, mostly based on what was annoying me about other readers.

The reader

A reader that respects the words.

Eight reading fonts including Literata, Lora, Merriweather, and Open Dyslexic. Eleven themes split between three light (Paper, Sepia, Solari) and eight dark variants like AMOLED, Nord, Dracula, and Catppuccin. Adjustable size, line height, margins.

Scroll mode for binge sessions, paged mode when you want to slow down. Long-press a paragraph to bookmark it or pull a highlight. Reading position is saved on every page turn — come back a week later and you'll be on the same line.

  • 11 themes, 8 fonts, paged or scrolled
  • Bookmarks, highlights, annotations
  • Position saved across restarts

The audio

Text-to-speech that sounds good.

Three engine options: your device's system TTS, plus neural voices from Piper and Kokoro that you download in-app when you want better quality. Speed, pitch, volume, sentence gaps, paragraph gaps — all separately adjustable.

The pronunciation dictionary fixes the words your engine keeps mangling — character names, place names, fictional terms. Save it as JSON and share between devices. Generate full M4B audiobooks with chapter markers when you want to take a novel running.

  • System TTS plus Piper and Kokoro neural voices
  • M4B audiobook export with chapter markers
  • Audio focus respects calls and other media

The stats

Your reading, in numbers.

Word counts, chapters, time spent, daily streaks. A 12-week activity heatmap. Personal records for longest session and best day. Week-over-week percentages so you can see whether you're trending up or just remembering selectively.

Everything's computed locally on your phone. There's no analytics service watching what you read. At the end of the week you can share a summary card if you want — same way Strava does post-workout images, just for books.

  • Daily, weekly, all-time stats
  • 12-week heatmap and streak tracking
  • Shareable summary cards

More inside

What's in there.

01

Works without signal.

Download chapters in batches and they're yours. EPUBs import in seconds. Reading state syncs back to the library on every page. Subway, flight, family dinner — same app.

02

Volume key navigation.

Volume up scrolls up, down scrolls down. Off by default. Audio focus is handled properly so phone calls and podcasts still work normally when you're not in the reader.

03

Pronunciation dictionaries.

The TTS keeps butchering "Aelindra"? Add it to the dictionary once and you're done. Export the whole dictionary as a single JSON file and load it on another device.

04

Bookmarks & highlights.

Long-press a paragraph to bookmark it with a note. Select text to highlight in six colors. Search across every bookmark in a novel. Export the lot as JSON for safekeeping.

05

EPUB import.

Drag any .epub into the app. Cover, chapters, metadata — extracted automatically. Local books and online novels live side by side in the same library.

06

Hands-free auto-scroll.

Teleprompter-style continuous drift down the page, speed adjustable from 20 to 200 pixels per second. Tap anywhere to pause. Auto-advances chapters with a beat at the top of each new one.

07

Continue-reading widget.

A 2×1 home screen widget that shows the cover and chapter you stopped on. Tap and you're back at the saved paragraph. Updates automatically as your reading position moves.

08

Quiet update checks.

The app checks for new chapters in the background when you're on wifi, then surfaces them the next time you open it. Off by default — turn it on if you want it.

About installing

Not on the Play Store.

The app ships as an APK you install yourself. Going through the Play Store would require an account integration, a developer fee, and ad SDK compliance, none of which fit a project like this.

Sideloading takes about thirty seconds. The trade-off is worth it.

  • No accounts You don't sign up. Ever.
  • No tracking Zero analytics, zero telemetry.
  • No ads Not now, not later.
  • Open source Read the code if you want to.
  • Your data Stays on your phone.

Questions

The honest FAQ.

Is it safe to install?

The APK is built from the open-source code on GitHub and signed with a stable key. If you want to verify it, every release page lists a SHA-256 — compare it against your downloaded file before installing.

Android shows a warning the first time you install anything from outside the Play Store. That warning shows for every sideloaded APK on the planet, not just this one.

What about updates?

The app checks the GitHub releases page once a day in the background and shows you a non-intrusive prompt when a new version is available. Updates are manual — tap, the APK downloads, you install. Same flow as the first install.

Don't want the prompt? Turn it off in Settings.

Where does the chapter content come from?

NovelForge connects to publicly-accessible sites you'd otherwise visit in a browser. The app fetches the page and displays it in a cleaner format. No content is hosted by NovelForge.

If a site asks readers to support authors via Patreon or a paid tier, please do. Web novelists rely on it. The app is a reading tool, not a piracy tool.

Will there ever be an iOS version?

Probably not. iOS doesn't allow apps like this to be installed without going through the App Store, and the App Store's rules around content scraping and indie distribution conflict with how the app works.

If Apple's EU-style sideloading expands worldwide, the calculation changes. For now, Android only.

Can I support the project?

Bug reports, feature suggestions, and code contributions on GitHub are the most useful thing. After that, telling other readers it exists.

There's no donation page on principle. If you're feeling generous, support a web novelist whose story you've enjoyed — they need it more than this project does.

What sources are supported?

Currently: RoyalRoad, ReadNovelFull, FreeWebNovel, LibRead, NovelFullNet, PawRead, and PrimordialTranslation. Plus EPUB import for any other novel you have as a file. New sources get added when users ask and when their scrapers prove stable.

Where is my data stored?

Locally, in the app's private storage on your device. Reading history, bookmarks, highlights, downloads, settings — all of it. The app never sends your reading data to a server because there is no NovelForge server.

For backups, Settings has an "Export backup" option that produces a single ZIP file you can save anywhere. Importing the ZIP on a new device restores everything.
