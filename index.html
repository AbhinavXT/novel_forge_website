I
Chapter I · the premise

Built for how you actually read.

Most reading apps assume you read in ten-minute commutes. This one assumes you're eleven hundred chapters into something,1 with four more books in rotation, and you just hit a name you haven't seen since last March. Other apps leave you to scroll back. NovelForge tells you who they are, when you met them, and every scene they've been in, without showing a single chapter you haven't reached.

The same idea runs through the whole app. It works offline by default, because you read on flights. It has twelve themes, because your eyes change as the day goes on. Your stats stay on your phone, so you can think "huh, I read more than I thought" without a dashboard somewhere thinking it too.2

The whole thing is free and the source is open. What's on your phone stays there unless you choose otherwise.3

¹ Not an exaggeration. Web serials run to thousands of chapters, and the app is tuned for libraries that size.
² There is no analytics SDK in the app, and no NovelForge server for one to report to.
³ The exceptions are all opt-in and listed in Chapter VIII.
II
Chapter II · the codex

"Wait, who is this again?" Solved.

Built for the question every long-series reader asks. Try it below: the codex follows the same bookmark as the graph up top, so move either one and see what you're allowed to know.

Character codex

Every character, place and faction, indexed on your phone.

NovelForge scans your downloaded chapters on the device and finds every recurring character, place and faction. Each entry shows when it first appeared and how often it turns up, with a presence sparkline across the book and every mention with a snippet. Tap a mention and the reader opens at that paragraph.

The spoiler guard is part of how the codex is built. Entries and mentions stop at your current chapter, so a character you haven't met doesn't exist yet, and one you have met can't leak a later scene.

  • First appearance, mention timeline, and the highlights you made about them
  • A "Previously in…" recap after a week away: where you stopped, and who's been around
  • Spoiler-safe by default, with a toggle for when you want to peek

    Relationship graph

    Who appears with whom, drawn instead of listed.

    The graph at the top of this page uses the same layout as the app. Characters are sized by how often they appear and linked when they share chapters, so factions cluster and loners drift to the edges. Tap two characters to get every chapter they share, then tap a chapter to read the scene.

    The graph only uses chapters behind your bookmark, so it grows as you read. Finishing an arc redraws the map.

    • A force-directed map of the story so far
    • Tap any two characters for their shared scenes
    • Pinch to zoom; it updates as your position moves
    Optional · with your own AI key

    Ask about a character.

    If you've set up an AI provider with your own key, each codex entry gets an Ask about … box. The answer is written only from passages in chapters you've already read, and nothing past your place in the book is sent. Without a key, the box doesn't appear.

    ✦Ask about Ilse: "Why doesn't she trust the Ferryman?" The answer can only draw on chapters 34–140.

    Chapter III · search & margins

    Your library, greppable.

    Full-text search

    "Where did I read that?" takes two seconds.

    Every downloaded chapter is indexed on the device. Type a name, a phrase or half a memory, and results come back grouped by book with the match marked in context. Tap one and the reader opens at that paragraph, not just at the start of the chapter.

    Inside a chapter, a find bar does the same at close range. It marks every occurrence, has next and previous, and works in scroll and paged mode.

    • Search every downloaded chapter at once
    • Jump straight to the matching paragraph
    • In-chapter find with match highlighting
    Margins

    Highlights, bookmarks, and a way back to them.

    Long-press a paragraph to bookmark it with a note, or select text to highlight it in one of six colours. Everything is searchable, highlights and notes export as Markdown, and any highlight can become a quote card to share. The codex cross-references your highlights too, so opening a character shows every line you've marked about them.

    Reading history keeps the trail of every session and every book, so "the thing I was reading two Tuesdays ago" is something the app can actually answer.

    • Six highlight colours, notes on bookmarks
    • Markdown export; quote cards in three looks
    • Session-level reading history

    V
    Chapter V · the audio

    Every character gets their own voice.

    Text-to-speech

    Three engines, a cast of voices, one pronunciation dictionary.

    Your phone's own text-to-speech works out of the box, and you can download Piper and Kokoro neural voices in the app when you want better. They run on the device. Give the narrator one voice and each character another, and dialogue starts to sound like a cast. Speed, pitch, volume and the gaps between sentences and paragraphs all adjust separately.

    The pronunciation dictionary fixes the names and invented words your engine keeps mangling. It can also silence the stray asterisks and scene-break marks that would otherwise be read out as "asterisk". Save it as JSON and load it on another device. When a book deserves a long walk, export it as an M4B audiobook with chapter markers.

    • System voices, plus Piper and Kokoro neural voices on-device
    • Character voices for dialogue
    • M4B audiobook export with chapter markers
    • Sleep timer, and audio focus that respects calls and other media

    Chapter VI · the numbers

    Your reading, kept honest.

    Stats

    Words, streaks and records, counted on your phone.

    The stats screen tracks words, chapters, time and streaks, with a 12-week heatmap and week-over-week changes so you can see whether you're trending up or just remembering selectively. Year in review sums up each calendar year, with your busiest month and your most-read books.

    All of it is computed on the device. There's no analytics service watching what you read, and no server for one to run on. Share a summary card if you want to; that's your call, one card at a time.

    • Daily, weekly and all-time stats, with records and streaks
    • Year in review, with shareable 1080×1920 cards

    VII
    Chapter VII · everything else inside

    The rest of the shelf.

    Eleven more things the app does. Pull a book off the shelf.

    Library

    EPUB, text or Markdown in. EPUB out.

    Import any .epub, .txt or .md, or point the app at a folder and let it find them. The cover, chapters and metadata are pulled out automatically, and chapter breaks are detected from headings, chapter lines or scene breaks. Any book exports back to a clean EPUB, so your files stay yours.

    Sources

    Web sources, as plugins.

    Add web sources by installing plugins from a repository. They update without waiting for a new app release. Chapters download into your library for offline reading, with a downloads queue and an option to delete chapters after you've read them. NovelForge itself hosts and distributes nothing.

    Reading modes

    Scroll, page, or drift.

    Infinite scroll for binge nights. Paged mode with slide, fade or page-curl transitions, or none, for when you want to slow down. Teleprompter auto-scroll from 20 to 200 px/s for hands-free reading. Any book can keep its own look.

    Controls

    Taps where your thumb is.

    Five tap-zone layouts: Sides, Top/Bottom, Forward (tap anywhere to advance), L-shaped, or Off for swipe purists. The zones match in both reading modes. Volume keys can turn pages too; that's off by default.

    Translation

    Read it in your language.

    Translate chapters with an offline on-device translator, one download per language pair, or with an AI provider using your own key. The original text is kept, so you can switch back.

    Words

    Dictionary, vocabulary, flashcards.

    Select a word and tap Define. Offline StarDict dictionaries are checked first, with no connection needed. Save the word and it keeps the definition, the book and the sentence it came from, ready for spaced-repetition flashcards.

    Text replacement

    Fix the book without touching it.

    Rules that change what the reader shows. Settle a name a translation spells three ways, swap a term you'd rather read differently, or remove a site watermark pasted into every chapter.

    Collections

    A library that stays sorted.

    Categories you define, an updates feed for ongoing books, grid or list views, and a home screen that knows what you were reading and how far you got, down to the paragraph.

    Portability

    Back up in one file. Sync without an account.

    Library, history, bookmarks, highlights and settings save to a single backup file, on a schedule if you like. On a new phone, import it once and you're done. To keep two devices in step, sync through a WebDAV folder you control, such as Nextcloud or a NAS.

    Privacy

    App lock and incognito.

    Lock the app behind your fingerprint, face or PIN. It asks when the app starts and when you come back after a minute away, and blanks its card in Recents on Android 13 and later. Incognito mode lets you read off the record.

    Reading comfort

    Type that fits your eyes.

    Eight bundled fonts, including OpenDyslexic, plus any TTF or OTF you import. Bionic reading, justified or ragged text, paragraph indents, adjustable margins, and a custom theme with your own colours.

    Chapter VIII · where your reading goes

    Free, and it stays that way.

    NovelForge is a free download from GitHub. There's no paid tier, no subscription and no ads, now or later. You never make an account. The character analysis that looks like it should need a cloud doesn't use one. Here is the full list of what stays on your phone and what can leave it.

    Stays on your phone

    always
    • Your library and chaptersBooks, downloads, covers.
    • Codex, graph and searchBuilt and indexed on the device.
    • Stats, history, highlightsComputed and kept locally.
    • Neural voicesPiper and Kokoro run on the device.

    Leaves only if you ask

    opt-in, to places you choose
    • Web sourcesFetched from the sites you add, the way a browser would.
    • AI extrasTranslation, recaps and "Ask about" go to your provider with your key, and only for chapters you've read.
    • SyncTo your own WebDAV folder. Chapter text stays on each device.
    • Online definitionsOnly when no offline dictionary has the word.

    Never

    not now, not later
    • AccountsYou don't sign up. Ever.
    • Analytics or telemetryNo tracking SDKs in the app.
    • Ads or in-app purchasesNo tiers, no upsells.
    • A NovelForge serverThere isn't one to send anything to.

    Appendix · honest answers

    The honest FAQ.

    Is it really free?
    Yes. It's free to download from GitHub Releases, with no subscription, ads or in-app purchases. The source is open under the MIT license, so you can see exactly what it does.
    What is the character codex?
    The app scans your downloaded chapters on the device and builds an index of recurring characters, places and factions: when they first appeared, how often they turn up, and every mention with a snippet. It only shows what comes before your reading position, so it can't spoil you. Tap any mention and the reader opens at that paragraph.
    Does the character analysis use the cloud?
    No. The codex, the relationship graph and full-text search all run on your phone, using fast heuristics and a local index rather than an API call. A few extras are optional: AI translation, "The story so far" recaps and "Ask about a character". They only work if you add your own AI key, they talk straight to that provider, and they only send chapters you've already read.
    What books can I read in it?
    Anything you have as an EPUB, plus plain text and Markdown files. Import one and the cover, chapters and metadata are extracted automatically. You can also add web sources as plugins. NovelForge doesn't host, store or distribute any content itself, and anything in your library exports back out as a clean EPUB.
    Can I sync between two devices?
    Yes, without an account. Sync goes through a WebDAV folder you provide, such as Nextcloud, a NAS or any WebDAV host. Chapter text isn't uploaded; each device keeps its own downloads.
    Where is my data stored?
    Locally, in the app's private storage: reading history, bookmarks, highlights, codex and settings. Settings has a one-tap backup that saves everything to a single file, and it can run on a schedule. Importing that file on a new device restores it all.
    Will it run on my phone?
    If it runs Android 7.0 or later on a 64-bit processor, yes. That covers almost every phone sold in the last several years. Older 32-bit-only devices can't install it.
    Will there ever be an iOS version?
    It's not planned. An iOS version would need a separate codebase and a different way of distributing it, and the focus is on making the Android app as good as it can be.
    Can I support the project?
    The most useful things are bug reports, feature ideas and code contributions on GitHub. After that, tell other readers it exists. There's no donation page on principle; if you're feeling generous, support an author whose work you've enjoyed.
